Give change a place in the wider enterprise.

For technology leaders managing an interconnected application estate, enterprise architecture links investment choices to business capabilities, information flows and long-term ownership.

THE PROBLEM IN CONTEXT

Understand the consequences before adding another system

A new platform changes more than one project. It can duplicate customer records, create another integration to maintain or shift operational responsibility. An enterprise view makes those consequences visible while there is still time to change direction.

Capability maps

Connect business activities to the applications and teams that support them, exposing gaps and overlapping responsibilities.

Architecture decision records

Capture alternatives, constraints and consequences so future teams can understand significant platform choices.

Transition states

Describe the intermediate arrangements needed while legacy and replacement systems operate together.

WHAT THE WORK INVOLVES

Enterprise Architecture
in practice.

  1. Trace dependencies

    Identify which processes depend on shared identities, records, interfaces and infrastructure.

  2. Set architectural boundaries

    Agree which capabilities belong in which systems and how teams should exchange information.

  3. Sequence the transition

    Organise changes around dependencies and retirement opportunities instead of treating every programme as an isolated purchase.

ILLUSTRATIVE SCENARIO · NOT A CLIENT CASE STUDY

An example: reducing application overlap

Where several departments maintain their own customer databases, architecture work can establish an authoritative record, define permitted extensions and plan the transition without requiring one immediate replacement.

BEFORE AN ENGAGEMENT

Questions about Enterprise Architecture.

Discuss this scope ↗
Does architecture require replacing our existing platforms?

No. Retaining, containing, integrating and retiring are all valid options. The recommendation should reflect business value and operational constraints.

How do you stop architecture becoming shelfware?

Tie decisions to funded initiatives, identify owners and revisit the design when a material assumption changes. The diagrams should support decisions people actually need to make.
